# Prism 2.0.2: Speedpatch

What was reported after 2.0: it lags badly, the GPU load is high, the text looks smeared, the first seconds of a song sometimes wait before they play, the library feels slow to load, the installed icon is blank, and the song title under the cover should be bigger.

## Diagnosis

| # | What you saw | What it was | How it was confirmed | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lag that gets worse the longer you listen | **A leak in the seek bar.** Every play-state change and every new track started another animation loop that never ended while playing. After a few tracks dozens of loops rewrote the seek bar 60 times a second each, and the visualisers had to wait their turn behind them. | Measured on 2.0.0 in a test browser: 1 loop after 1 track, about 13 after 5 tracks, about 20 after 10. The page's frame rate was 2 to 19 frames a second. With the fix: 1 loop after 14 tracks and 36 to 49 frames a second. | One loop only, driven by a 66 ms timer (about 15 updates a second). The text and the bar are only written when they change. The lyrics follower got the same treatment. |
| 2 | GPU load | **Several things, all of them constant.** (a) The blurred album art behind the window was a CSS `blur(70px)` filter: whenever anything drawn over it changed (a visualiser frame, the seek bar, the playing-row bars) the graphics card redid that blur. (b) The glass, frosted and background-blur looks add a `backdrop-filter` on every panel. (c) Every visualiser bar drew its own glow (a Gaussian blur per bar, per frame), at up to 60 frames a second. (d) Every frame each visualiser asked the page for its size, which forces a layout. (e) Visualisers kept drawing while the window was minimised. (f) The playing-row equaliser animated `height` (layout and paint every frame). | Code review plus the measurements below. | (a) The picture is blurred once per cover on a small canvas and used as a plain image. (b) The see-through looks and the background blur are gone. (c) Glow only at the *Smooth* quality level; the default, *Balanced*, runs at 30 frames a second without glow. *Light* runs at 20. (d) A size observer. (e) Nothing is drawn while minimised, and at most 15 frames a second behind another window (not at Smooth). (f) The bars animate `transform`. |
| 3 | Smeared text (your screenshot) | Text that sits on a layer with a filter, a backdrop blur, a mask or a scale transform is drawn into an intermediate picture and resampled. With display scaling above 100% (125%, 150%) that is visibly soft, and that is what the whole-window smear in your screenshot looks like. Your panels were see-through, so a blur look was on. | Not reproducible on the Linux test machine (no display scaling there), so this one is a diagnosis, not a measurement. The screenshot, the see-through panels and the code agree. | No `backdrop-filter` is left anywhere. The current lyric line is bolder instead of scaled. The window background and the fullscreen lyrics background are plain images. |
| 4 | A wait before a song starts, sometimes | At the moment you press play, Prism also started pre-decoding the next three tracks (one ffmpeg each, plus reading their files). On a hard disk that competes with the track you just started. On top of that, the server thread that feeds the audio was sometimes busy: it turned the whole library into JSON on every request, wrote big files synchronously, redid the Last.fm play-count matching on every settings save, and re-checked every file one after another. | Code review; the effects are in the tests below. | Pre-decoding waits 3 seconds after a track starts (a quick series of skips prepares only the last few). Background ffmpeg jobs run at a lower priority. The library JSON is made once per version. Files are saved in the background. The play-count matching and the recap numbers are remembered until something changes. The browser audio engine is built at start-up instead of at the first press of Play. |
| 5 | Slow reading of storage / songs | At every start the whole library was checked again (one file after another), then the interface downloaded and rebuilt the entire list even when nothing had changed. A scan that found nothing still rewrote the library file. | Tests in `speed.test.js`. | Files are checked in parallel. The first re-check happens 4 seconds after the start, not at the moment the first song may be starting. A scan or a drop that found nothing changes nothing: no file rewrite, no new version, no download. Changes found later (including by the folder watcher) are picked up by a check every 15 seconds. |
| 6 | Blank icon after install | The 2.0.1 icon was a hand-made .ico with a 256 px uncompressed picture, an unusual layout for Windows. The window also had no icon of its own to fall back on (the icon file was not inside the packaged app). Windows can also keep showing an old icon from its cache after an install. | Cannot be checked here (no Windows). | The program gets its icon from `build/icon.png`, converted by electron-builder in the standard way; the installer keeps the 2.0.1 `.ico` (it is known to build and run); the icon travels with the app and the window uses it; the installer asks Explorer to refresh its icon cache. The build now extracts the icon out of the finished `Prism.exe` and fails if it is blank (`diagnostics/exe-icon.png`). |

## Also in this patch
- The song title under the album cover is 22 px, bold (it was 15 px). The artist line is 13.5 px.
- Removed: **Frosted glass**, **Frosted pastel**, **Liquid** and the **Background blur** setting (menus, settings and code). A saved choice of one of them becomes its solid sibling: glass and liquid become Flat, frosted pastel becomes Pastel.
- New: **Visualiser quality** (Settings > Appearance, or View > Visualiser quality). Default: Balanced.

## Tests
- Unit tests: 263 pass (255 before; `speed.test.js` adds 7, one more for the window-visibility signal).
- Browser tests: `python test/e2e_speed.py` (55 checks) covers everything above that can be measured. The browser tests for the removed looks and blur were cut from `e2e_v13`, `e2e_v14` and `e2e_v20`.

## Not verified (no Windows machine here)
- How sharp the text is on a real display with scaling, and the real GPU load. The numbers above come from a software-rendered test browser, so only the *ratios* mean something.
- The real icon in Explorer and the taskbar, and the installer with the new icon setup and the cache-refresh step (the build checks both and will say so).
- First play on a real hard disk.
